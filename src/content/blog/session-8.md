Having survived their encounter with Fresnelkesh, the party resumed their exploration of the Gauntlight. Bonk remained to guard the workmen, while Nin led the others back through the passage below Volluk’s workshop. Their first encounter on the new level was a pile of enormous bones beginning to assemble itself into a skeletal giant. Erzsebet recognised it as a mindless undead creature incorporating minotaur bones, and the party prepared themselves for a fight. The skeletal giant proved as formidable as its appearance suggested, sweeping an enormous rusty glaive across the room, nearly killing several members of the party in a single devastating attack. Its resistance to slashing and piercing weapons appeared insurmountable, but the party adapted quickly. The party took its head as a trophy.

The party dressed their wounds and proceeded cautiously, entering a small washroom whose northern wall glowed with eerie green light. The energy condensed into a message in Common: “I was Otari. Save me from below!” The words then detached themselves from the wall and transformed into a ribbon of green light which slithered away into the depths. The name immediately caught the party’s attention, since Otari Ilvashti had been one of the Roseguard heroes responsible for defeating Belcorra. The trail disappeared towards the deeper levels, however, and the party elected not to follow it immediately.

Instead they proceeded into a small hallway containing a series of dioramas depicting the city of Absalom facing a succession of catastrophes: a fire, a tsunami, an earthquake, an attack by an enormous tentacled monster and finally an invasion of undead. Genevieve, who had lived in Absalom, recognised the city in its ancient form, while the final scene was particularly unsettling, showing undead rising from the graves within the city itself. The first diorama abruptly discharged a devastating blast of fire when Nin approached, severely wounding her and revealing that the hallway was protected by an elaborate series of magical hazards. Quintus established that the traps would eventually reset, but the party devised a more expedient solution than attempting to disarm them individually: Erzsebet sent expendable blood thralls through the hall to deliberately trigger each hazard. Before long every trap had discharged, leaving the hallway safe to cross for roughly an hour. The party then quickly administered a slew of healing potions to Nin and hurried onward before the magical defences could reset.

Beyond the trap-filled hall they found a large web-covered war room containing an illusory map of ancient Absalom. There they encountered the chamber’s occupant, a web lurker who styled himself Lord Mimnivix, King of the Ghost City. Mimnivix demanded that the party acknowledge his authority and present him with offerings, but the party instead attempted to exploit his vanity. Their diplomacy proved surprisingly effective, and Mimnivix agreed to treat them as his servants. Detect Magic revealed a substantial hoard of treasure concealed beneath the table, and the party began negotiating for access to what Mimnivix referred to as his “crown jewels.” An initial attempt failed, but the party eventually offered him a trade, convincing the self-proclaimed king to surrender the contents of his web-wrapped hoard in exchange for being allowed to keep Erzsebet as his dinner guest.

Mimnivix surrendered a mesmerising opal, five shining sling bullets and a Type I necklace of fireballs. The party then feigned to withdraw while leaving Erzsebet behind, but the web lurker and his spiders couldn’t resist their ostensible meal. Erzsebet was wrapped in webs and seized by the creature while additional spiders swarmed over her, forcing the rest of the party to intervene.

The party searched the room before leaving, although they failed to detect the concealed web lurker nooses that had nearly caught them on several occasions. They secured the treasures Mimnivix had surrendered and made a rapid retreat through the recently cleared trap corridor before its magical defences could reset. Back in the safer rooms they healed and prepared to continue their exploration.

The party’s spectacularly noisy progress through the trapped processional hallway, followed by their confrontation with Lord Mimmivix, eventually attracted another resident of the Gauntlight. As they emerged from the web lurker’s chamber, a hideous winged devil calling himself Zozzlarin burst from a nearby doorway, furious at the party for disturbing him. The creature immediately summoned a swarm of rats and retreated down the corridor, leaving the rats to engulf the party. The swarm’s filthy bites and diseased bodies proved more dangerous than their actual attacks, and Laurah was exposed to Putrid Plague as the rats harried the group. Zozzlarin’s own defences initially made him extremely difficult to hurt: he was resistant to ordinary physical and poison damage and immune to fire. Genevieve quickly identified the importance of silver. The combination proved decisive. The furious devil was finally forced to surrender and beg for his life.

He transmitted a perfect recollection of a scene from the Gauntlight’s past: an adventurer racing through the complex while ancient ballistae fired upon him, effortlessly dodging their attacks, destroying supernatural enemies on the run, dispatching a Minotaur with a single blow, and somehow spotting the concealed entrance into Belcorra’s study before diving through it and disappearing into the depths. The party immediately recognised the man as Otari Ilvashti. Zozzlarin’s memory showed that he had still been alive when he made this journey, and revealed him to have been an extraordinarily accomplished adventurer. Having provided the party with the most interesting event he had witnessed in centuries, Zozzlarin retreated to his bedroom. Its walls painted with crude scenes of Hell, burning mountains and tormented souls by its homesick occupant, and resumed his lonely existence, leaving the party free to explore his quarters. One room contained a silver-headed cane concealed among mops and cleaning equipment, which proved to be a low-grade silver sword cane, while another housed two enormous clockwork assistants. Nin’s engineering knowledge revealed that the constructs had been designed for heavy labour and contained gemstone bearings.

Having finished their business with Zozzlarin, the party followed in reverse the route by which Otari Ilvashti had fled centuries before, eventually reaching another section of the servants’ quarters. Within, something was sawing, drilling and welding, oblivious to the chaos elsewhere in the complex. Genevieve crept forward to listen at the door, but the occupants detected her presence and responded immediately: a salvaged ballista fired from within, the massive bolt blasting through the doorway and wounding her. As the ruined door fell open, the party could see the source of the activity — two Morlock engineers working on the ancient ballistae that had once formed part of the Gauntlight’s defensive system. The machines that had harried Otari Ilvashti in Zozzlarin’s memory were now being repaired by the Morlocks. With Genevieve wounded by the sudden barrage and the two engineers now alerted to their presence, the party braced themselves for another fight.
